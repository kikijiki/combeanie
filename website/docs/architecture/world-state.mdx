# World state

The C++ world-state store is the inventory and task authority. It tracks objects
(id, class, SKU, pose, covariance, grasp/tracking state), lanes (compatibility, depth,
obstruction, semantic `contents`), robot execution semantics, revisions, and reservations.

## Trust boundaries

<TrustBoundaryDiagram />

| Boundary                     | Owns                                                                              | Must not                               |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **World state**              | Product identity, lane `contents`, task/fault, attachment semantics, reservations | Learned inference                      |
| **MoveIt planning scene**    | Collision geometry for planning                                                   | Act as inventory database              |
| **Perception / models**      | Observations and advisory suggestions                                             | Joint commands, unchecked trajectories |
| **Gazebo attachment plugin** | Multi-tick physical coupling evidence                                             | Accept world-state tokens directly     |

Discovery is not authority. On every coordinator start, a snapshot probe must prove the world is
unreserved, idle, fault-free, and empty-handed before goals are admitted.

## Evidence vs contents

Lane **evidence** (depth, obstruction, source IDs) may refresh from perception. Semantic
`contents` change only through reservation-authorized attachment/detachment commits and through
the world state's own FIFO ledger reconciliation of an accepted survey (below). That split
is why MoveIt is not used as a stock database.

Object evidence refreshes the same way, with one reservation-scoped rule: while a reservation is
at its pre-attach `Reserved` stage, the **reserved** product's pose is held. Fresh observations
still advance its stamp, tracking state, identity, revision and events, but `pose_in_world` is
replaced only when the observation is farther than `reserved_pose_divergence_bound_m`
(default **0.020 m**) from the held pose — inside that bound, confirm-frame noise cannot rewrite
the collision object under a grasp that was staged once and frozen; beyond it the new pose is
applied immediately, so a product that really moved reaches the planning scene instead of being
hidden. The hold ends at attach and at reservation release.

## Lane ledger and deficits

A gravity-fed lane is a FIFO column: placements push at the back, customers pop at the front.
Each accepted lane survey reconciles the measured column length (`depth_m - available_depth_m`)
against the settled contents at the catalogued product pitch:

- Agreement within **half a pitch** — nothing changes. An in-flight Attached placement counts
  only toward the growth bound, so pre-insert surveys at the old column length neither invent a
  sale nor withdraw trust.
- Shorter than the settled contents by one or more whole pitches — that many **front entries pop
  as sales**; the sold objects are marked removed so they stop counting as stock.
- Growth beyond what the contents plus an in-flight placement can explain, or a shortfall with no
  whole front-entry sale to pop — the ledger is marked **unreliable**
  (`ShelfLane.ledger_unreliable`, surfaced as a `LANE_LEDGER_UNRELIABLE` snapshot event). Identity
  trust is withdrawn and latched; the measured geometry stands, so depth evidence, the
  occupied-volume projection, and geometry-safe placements continue.
- A ledger whose entries the current policy does not accept is left untouched by length
  reconciliation: the new intent's pitch does not describe the old column.

Deficits are computed from that geometry against the owner's want:
`held = round(column_length / pitch)`, `deficit = max(0, target_count - held)`. Task selection
routes against them: **largest deficit first, ties broken by rail distance from the carriage**,
then the existing front-row and travel-cost rules choose the product. A lane whose ledger names
products the current policy no longer accepts is reported and skipped (`lane_wrong_product`)
rather than routed into; a lane whose expected product has no catalogued pitch fails closed
instead of inheriting a sibling's.

## Lane intent

`expected_product_class`, `expected_sku`, and `target_count` on each `ShelfLane` are the owner's
declared want, loaded from `baseline_lanes.yaml` and editable at runtime through
`/world_state/set_lane_policy`. The service is revision-fenced: a stale expected revision or a
failed write changes neither the lane nor the persisted `lane_policy_state` document, which is
written atomically (temp file + fsync + rename) and overlaid on the baseline at the next startup.
A policy change bumps the lane revision and invalidates that lane's evidence until re-surveyed;
a reservation already granted keeps the destination policy it captured at grant, so in-flight
transfers finish under their original policy while new selections see the new intent.

Mutation replies carry opaque tokens that must never be logged or snapshotted. An orphaned
reservation permanently inhibits coordinator admission until the operator clears the cause;
there is no force-release shortcut.

## Attachment time and clock authority

Object capture timestamps remain source evidence, including the existing bounded allowance for
observations slightly ahead of their receipt time. An attachment never commits before the current
object transition or robot telemetry timestamp. If only an admitted object observation is ahead,
the store can return `ATTACHMENT_CLOCK_NOT_READY`: it requires matching object revision, original
receipt provenance, and the same active, started managed ROS clock lineage. It changes no world
revision, reservation stage, held identity, or journal entry. Ordinary system-time operation still
works; missing managed-clock provenance does not qualify for this retry disposition.

The node orders clock sampling and admission under the clock update mutex, then the store mutex.
A managed backward clock change or ROS override change inhibits temporal admission once live
evidence or a reservation exists. Static configured lanes alone do not arm that latch. Their configuration timestamp does not
fence the first actual observation; subsequent observations still require strictly newer evidence. Runtime
`use_sim_time` changes remain rejected; reconfiguration requires a controlled restart. This
lineage does not authenticate `/clock` publisher identity or detect every positive replacement
of that publisher.

Exact attachment replay includes the capability and every post-conversion transform coefficient.
Equivalent quaternion signs and signed zero produce the same transform identity; different finite
values conflict even below pose tolerances. After clock inhibition, an original committed receipt
is still available under `CLOCK_AUTHORITY_INHIBITED`, with its original revision and attached
stage. It is reconciliation evidence and never ordinary success. An OK reply produced before an
unseen later reset is outside this server-ordering guarantee.

`ValidateExecutionWorldAuthority` also refuses while clock authority is inhibited. It currently
has no production motion consumer, so that API check is not a motion-dispatch fence. Coordinated
motion ownership, queued commands and restart recovery need their own authority boundary.

## Journal capacity and cleanup

Operation journals retain accepted IDs and results for replay; they do not evict old records to
admit new work. A reservation needs room for reserve, semantic attach, semantic detach, and release
before admission. Checkpoints use only capacity beyond the remaining lifecycle entries. Thus a
checkpoint cannot consume the entries needed to cancel or settle an admitted task, and semantic
attachment cannot run out of journal space after physical attachment has succeeded. All existing
identity, revision, evidence, and terminal-outcome checks still apply to cleanup.

The ROS attachment adapter and simulator have separate journals. Each protects the configured
`detach_attempt_reserve` when admitting attach (**three attempts** in the shipped boundary
configuration). At the adapter, only detaches for the held object under its original reservation
capability may use protected entries. Detach acquires a fresh scene lease, which is checked by
live authorization. Other requests, including rejected authorization attempts from another owner,
can consume only unreserved capacity. Exact replays consume no additional entries.

The retry allowance is finite: each distinct admitted owner detach consumes an attempt, including
one rejected by authorization or physical validation. After those attempts and ordinary free
entries are exhausted, a new detach is refused; retained results remain queryable and the held
object still requires explicit reconciliation. The journal does not authorize a force release.
Safe attach rejection and verified detach free unused reserved entries, while preserving their
operation records. New tasks eventually receive `ResourceExhausted` as retained history fills;
coordinated journal rollover and restart recovery are not implemented.

### Retention bounds and the read-only snapshot

Each journal states its bound and exports a read-only **retention snapshot** (no tokens, no
payloads) so that the bound can be asserted rather than inferred. A snapshot reports the journal
name, `capacity`, `size`, `open_obligations`, `terminal_receipts`, `reserved_credits`, an
`epoch_id`, an `inhibited` flag, and whether the journal is `evicting` (drops its oldest record
when full). Reading a snapshot never changes admission, eviction or replay behavior.

`open_obligations` counts **journal records only**: a record is open while it carries an
unsettled side effect, a reserved credit, or an unknown outcome, and terminal once it is settled
and kept only so an exact retry can replay, so `open_obligations + terminal_receipts = size`.
An obligation that is carried as state without a record is reported by a separate explicit field,
so `open_obligations == 0` never means "nothing owed". `reserved_credits` are entries held back for
settlement and are not part of `size`. The state-carried signals are `held_attachment` (simulator
and adapter: a physical attachment is held, whether or not a record still references it) and
`active_lease_phase` (planner lease: `none`, `draining`, `held` or `releasing`).

Within an epoch no operation journal evicts either kind of record: a full journal refuses new
admission instead. There is no age-based expiry.

| Journal                | Bound                               | Open record                                                                                              | State-carried signal                                       | `inhibited` means                                                                  | Epoch / restart                                                                                  |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| World-state operations | `operation_journal_capacity` (4096) | Entries of the active reservation until its release                                                      | `reserved_credits` (3, 2, 1 by stage, else 0)              | The store's clock authority is latched after a discontinuity                       | Per-process `epoch_id` (diagnostic only); empty after restart                                    |
| World-state events     | `event_capacity` (256)              | None (audit records, all counted terminal)                                                               | None                                                       | Same single clock flag as the operations journal                                   | Evicts oldest; empty after restart                                                               |
| Adapter operations     | Adapter journal capacity (64)       | Any non-terminal or inconsistent record, and the attach record that owns the held object's detach credit | `reserved_credits`, `held_attachment`                      | Adapter motion is inhibited; true until startup reconciliation proves a clean state | Per-process `epoch_id`; empty after restart; `initialize` re-derives inhibition                  |
| Simulator attachment   | Boundary journal capacity (64)      | Any in-flight, inconsistent or unknown record, and the attach record backing a held object               | `reserved_credits`, `held_attachment` (survives rotation)  | The attachment motion gate is not valid                                            | `simulator_epoch`; records are cleared on rotation, a held object stays visible as state         |
| Planner scene lease    | `operation_journal_capacity` (4096) | Acquire and release entries of the active lease                                                          | `reserved_credits` (2, 1, 0), `active_lease_phase`         | No verified, clean scene proof exists. A freshly constructed, idle lease is inhibited; this is not a fault | `projector_epoch`; a new process has no lease and old tokens are invalid                         |

Operation IDs are scoped to one authority and one epoch; they are not durable identities. After a
process restart the journals are empty and an old ID is answered as unknown, never as success: a
caller that survives a peer restart must treat the miss as an unknown prior outcome and reconcile
against physical and semantic state. The `epoch_id` of the world-state store and the adapter is a
diagnostic value, not a wire field. A clock discontinuity latches the clock authority but is not a
new epoch.
