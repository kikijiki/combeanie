# Stock and inventory

Two optional nodes read the lane survey and say how much of what is in stock. Both are readers:
`LaneObservation`, `ShelfLane.contents` (the ledger) and every existing consumer are untouched,
and nothing consumes their output. Both cores are pure Python with no ROS import; only the node
modules use ROS.

```bash
ros2 launch restocker_bringup baseline.launch.py lane_stock:=true inventory:=true
inventory_tool query --sku GEN-001
```

## Lane stock

`restocker_perception.stock` states, per lane, what is in it and how many: a count, a composition
against the lane policy, free depth, coverage and an explicit cannot-tell. `lane_stock_node`
publishes `LaneStock` on `/perception/lane_stock` from the survey's wrist frame. The wrist survey
frame looks down the lane from its rear entrance, so it shows the rearmost product and hides the
rest.

```text
wrist survey frame (depth, RGB, lane <- optical at the frame's stamp)
  -> lane depth histogram (the same window and thresholds as lane_depth_measurement.cpp)
  -> free depth, coverage, obstruction
  -> top-height staircase: the rear product's class, runs of equal products
  -> count = occupied depth over the contact pitch, cross-checked against the top surface
  -> crop of the rear product -> /identify_product -> naming policy
  -> LaneStock
```

### Count

A column is packed against the front retainer. With `n` upright products of one class the nearest
surface lies at `bed_datum - r - extent - (n-1) * pitch`. The estimator inverts it and takes the
nearest whole number. It reports an exact count when the column length is within 0.2 pitch of a whole
number, a one-product range within 0.35 pitch, and a refusal (`REASON_INCONSISTENT_GEOMETRY`) beyond
that. A cross-check counts the bins of the top surface in units of the pitch; if the two disagree by
more than 1.2 products the estimator refuses.

### Products and orientations

A product is a pair the formulas need: half its extent along the lane (`r`) and its height. A
standing cylinder is `(radius, height)`. A box is `size_xyz_m = (across, along, up)` with
`r = along / 2`, and a box resting on another face is another pair (its six faces).

- The lane policy names a class and a SKU. Class 4 (box) covers every box shape, so the SKU's
  registered shape decides what the lane expects.
- The rear top height is matched against the catalogue: every product standing, every orientation
  of the lane's expected product except its quarter turns.
- A quarter turn (the registered box turned about the vertical) has the same top height and another
  side along the lane. Height cannot tell it from the registered orientation, and under a few
  millimetres of noise a 55 mm pitch fits a 25 mm column by chance. Lanes are packed with the depth
  side along them, so a box turned in a lane is outside what the estimator counts, and it is not
  detected.
- When several candidates fit by height, the lane's own product is the answer; the estimator
  refuses if it does not count the column in whole products. Another product is named only when the
  lane's own is not among the candidates, by the column length in metres against the noise, and two
  that fit and count differently are refused as ambiguous.
- A column of another shape than expected is `class_mismatch` (misplaced), even when both are boxes.
- A column whose top height steps (a bottle in a can lane) is counted run by run and flagged as
  misplaced.
- A surface that tops out lower than every catalogued product is refused, not counted: through
  range noise the bed's own speckle reads as a product at the lane mouth.

### Cannot-tell

`STATE_CANNOT_TELL` always comes with a reason, never a low-confidence number: the producer refused
(`REASON_NO_EVIDENCE`), coverage is below the survey's 0.90 floor (`REASON_LOW_COVERAGE`), something
protrudes from the mouth, no class or geometry explains the column, or the top surface disagrees with
the depth count. A lane seen to be empty along its whole length is an answer
(`COMPOSITION_EMPTY`, count 0); a lane that was not seen is not. All count and composition fields of
a refusal are zero and must be ignored.

The estimator follows the noise of the frame. The floor margin is `max(10 mm, |bed offset| + 4
robust sigmas)` of the bed's own height residuals; the nearest surface moves back by 4 sigmas. A
surface under three bins wide (not a product) and a step in the top height shorter than a product
are refused. Above 8 mm of bed sigma the estimator reads no class and makes no class claim.

### Identity and composition

Identity is a possibly-abstaining input. The estimator calls `/identify_product` on the rear
product's crop and applies a **safe naming policy**: a name is accepted only when label text or the
barcode confirms the same GTIN; anything the embedding alone says is an abstention. With the identity
node absent, unreachable, or the face too small to ask about, the count is reported and
`identity_channel_reachable` says what happened.

Composition is not a boolean. `COMPOSITION_CONFORMING` needs every product named and expected,
`PARTIAL` some, `MISPLACED` a named wrong SKU or a class the policy does not hold (set only from
evidence, never from an abstention), `UNKNOWN` none. `unverified_count` is the number of products
nobody named. A survey frame shows the rear product and hides the rest, so at most one product per
lane can be named, and **an unflagged lane is not a verified-clean lane**.

## Inventory

`restocker_perception.inventory` aggregates the lane stock estimates and the tray into one
queryable inventory: per-SKU totals with their uncertainty spelled out, per-lane contents, the age of
every reading against a declared horizon, and a comparison with the authoritative world state.
`inventory_node` publishes `/perception/inventory` and serves `/query_inventory`.

### What a total means

A SKU's total is the sum of three kinds of product, kept apart in the message:

| Kind | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **confirmed** | The identity channel named the product. Only this is evidence of the SKU. |
| **assumed** | Counted by the sensor in a lane whose policy lists the SKU and not named individually; a range, the estimator's count range. |
| **reported** | Listed by the world state only (the tray's fallback). |

The assumed share can hide a wrong product behind the rear one, so the total's range covers the
count, not the SKU. A lane flagged misplaced is contested: its unnamed products are not assumed to be
the policy SKU (that share's lower end is zero), and a class mismatch with no name puts the wrong
products in an unknown-SKU bucket. A product the identity channel positively said is unregistered goes
to that bucket too.

### What is left out

A lane that was refused, never read, or last read longer ago than the horizon contributes nothing to
any total and makes the SKUs whose policy lane it is `complete = false`, with the lane named
(`unread_lanes`, `stale_lanes`). A SKU is complete only when every lane that could hold it was read
and fresh and the tray is covered. The horizon is a deployment parameter (`stale_after_s`, default
900 s; the per-query `max_age_s` overrides it), not a measured constant.

### Tray

The tray comes from the perception path when it has a fresh reading (`tray_observation_topic`, off by
default): each `ObjectObservation` is `IDENTIFIED` and so confirmed, `UNKNOWN_PRODUCT`, or not assessed
and so assumed from the manifest. Otherwise it comes from the world state (tracked free objects that no
lane's ledger or fresh occupancy accounts for), where it is reported, not sensed. In simulation the
world state is fed ground truth by default, so a world-sourced tray is the simulator's belief.

### Discrepancy

Each counted lane is compared with its ledger (`ShelfLane.contents`). A discrepancy is raised when the
observed count range lies above or below the ledger's count (`count_delta` says by how much), or when a
name the identity channel gave contradicts the SKU the ledger records for the rearmost product
(`sku_discrepancy`, never raised from an abstention). A lane that could not be compared is
`NOT_COMPARABLE`, which is not agreement. Per SKU, the world's figure and the difference are given when
the SKU is complete.

### Query

`QueryInventory` takes a SKU, a lane or neither, an evidence floor (`min_confidence`: `any`,
`expected` = sensor-counted only, `confirmed` = identity-named only) and a `max_age_s`. A SKU query
returns the count, its range, how much of it is confirmed, how many products the floor excluded, whether
the answer is complete and a sentence saying why. `inventory_tool` is the operator front end:

```text
inventory_tool query --sku GEN-001
inventory_tool query --sku GEN-001 --min-confidence confirmed
inventory_tool query --lane lane_03 --max-age 120
inventory_tool show
inventory_tool replay --datasets <pbench-dir>... --predictions <rows.jsonl> --seed 12 --sku GEN-001
```

With no identity channel running, every lane's SKU is the policy's: the counts are exact, the SKU is an
assumption, and a wrong product hidden behind the rear one is not seen. That is what the `confirmed` and
`assumed` split reports.
