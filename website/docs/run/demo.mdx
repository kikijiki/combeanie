# Demo

Fastest path to watch the robot restock:

```bash
just demo                # the showcase scene, on the ground-truth evidence path
just demo 3              # at most three campaign cycles
just demo sensor:=true   # the shipped sensor-driven path, on the three-product fixture
just demo gt:=true       # the dense plain-coloured fixture, on the ground-truth path
```

Each variant brings up Gazebo, MoveIt, RViz, and the coordinator, waits until the coordinator
reports ready, then starts the autonomous mode loop. At start of run the robot sweeps every
front-lane station with its wrist camera, measures each lane's deficit against the declared
target, and submits unaddressed `/restock_product` goals. A transfer goes out only after that
survey's close-range confirmation (or against an already-admitted candidate) while every lane's
evidence is inside its validity horizon. The loop parks in `IDLE` when every lane reaches its
target. Every start prints which evidence path it is on.

## Three scenes, two evidence paths

| Command | Scene | Evidence path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `just demo` | **Showcase.** Generated, labelled cans and bottles in five SKUs: six shelf lanes, some at target and some short, and a back room of packed one-SKU blocks. Eleven transfers fill the shelf. | Ground truth, pinned |
| `just demo sensor:=true` | **Simple fixture.** Three empty lanes and three tray products, one per class: three transfers, then `PHASE_FRONT_FULL` and an idle loop. The table the sensor-driven acceptance test proves. | Sensor-driven (the shipped configuration) |
| `just demo gt:=true` | **Dense fixture.** Six partly stocked lanes of plain-coloured cylinders and three dense-but-partial back grids: thirteen transfers. | Ground truth, pinned |

**The default uses the ground-truth path, and says so when it starts.** The sensor-driven path
identifies a product by its class: the camera identity assigner names a detection only when the
object manifest stocks exactly one product of that class (or, on the dense tray, by stocking
position). The showcase stocks several SKUs of every class side by side, so at tray and lane range
the camera cannot yet say which SKU a can is. Rather than fake an identity, the showcase pins
ground truth onto the ingest topics (both wrist tray duties off, one producer per topic): the
producer reports each product's declared SKU. Nothing else is simulated; motion, planning,
grasping and placement are the same on every path.

The sensor-driven path is the shipped configuration, the same defaults as a plain
`baseline.launch.py`: the wrist lane survey and the wrist tray-confirm duty own the world state's
ingest topics, simulator ground truth is published on `/perception/ground_truth/*` where only the
error evaluators read it, the evidence ages are the shipped 180 s profile, and the overhead camera
is on for **obstacle detection only** (its object pipeline is off). `just demo sensor:=true` pins
none of it. The ground-truth paths refresh evidence continuously, so they pin a tighter profile:
`product_observation_max_age` 2.0 s, `selection_object_max_age_ms` 2000,
`maximum_observation_age_ms` 2000 and `lane_evidence_validity_ms` 60000.

## The showcase scene

The showcase uses the generated catalogue's straight-cylinder SKUs (`tools/label_factory.py`).
Each carries a printed label (front panel, nutrition and barcode panel, a lid) and shares one of the
three base shapes' dimensions exactly, so every lane and tray formula applies unchanged. The
contoured bottles and the boxes are not in the scene: the grasp and placement stack handles
straight cylinders only.

- **Shelf.** Lanes 1 and 4 hold two can SKUs, lanes 2 and 5 two small-bottle SKUs, and lanes 3 and
  6 two facings of one large-bottle SKU. Columns stand against the front retainer with every label
  facing the shelf front. Lanes 2 and 6 start at their target; lanes 1, 3, 4 and 5 start two or
  three products short.
- **Back room.** One two-column block per SKU, five blocks left to right. Rows stand 12 mm apart;
  columns stand on the gripper's approach-corridor pitch, because the tool sweeps in along the
  tray's depth axis and a product beside the target has to clear the padded wrist. Every shelf SKU
  has a block. Each tray product stands at its own random yaw, fixed by its model name and a scene
  seed, so the labels face every direction as on delivered stock; a cylinder is symmetric about its
  axis, so the footprints and the pitch are those of an aligned block. Only the four SKUs with a
  deficit are picked, and each block keeps what its deficit does not use.
- **World.** `restocking_showcase.sdf` is the restocking world dressed as a cold-room store back: a
  concrete-slab floor texture with a safety line, panelled walls, a spot key light (its shadow map
  covers the cell instead of the whole room) and a shadowless fill. It carries the same physics and
  plugins as `restocking.sdf`; only the showcase selects it.
- **Materials.** The launch is given `bundle_scenario:=true` and `scene_materials:=…/showcase_materials.yaml`:
  the workcell and every product are written into the world file, so the window opens on the
  complete scene, and the workcell's visuals are restyled there (clear acrylic dividers, steel
  retainer and rollers, a teal tray). The workcell description keeps its flat colours for every
  test and benchmark; the robot keeps the description's materials.
- **Camera.** The Gazebo window opens on the `wide` view. The named views (`wide`, `shelf`,
  `shelf_closeup`, `tray`, `tray_closeup`, `action`, `side`) are listed in
  `website/scripts/showcase_views.py`, which also frames the recording and the screenshots.

The scenario and its lane policy are generated: `tools/generate_showcase_scenario.py` writes
`showcase_products.yaml` and `showcase_lanes.yaml`, and `--check` reports a stale pair.

## What you see

RViz opens with a predefined three-pane layout instead of the plain MoveIt window:

- **Centre — planning view.** The same MoveIt `MotionPlanning` display as
  [`just launch-baseline`](./scenarios): robot model, planning scene, fixed frame `world`.
- **Right, top — Overhead Camera.** The fixed shelf-facing RGB-D camera's colour stream on
  `/overhead_camera/image` (960x720, about 6 Hz): the whole shelf face, so you can watch deficits
  empty and transfers land. On every path this camera feeds obstacle detection, not product
  perception.
- **Right, bottom — Wrist Camera.** The gripper-mounted colour stream on
  `/wrist_camera/image` (1280x720, about 5 Hz): whatever the arm is looking at — lane survey
  stations and tray confirmation viewpoints as they happen. On the sensor-driven path this is the
  camera the evidence comes from.
- **Left — Displays and Views panels**, docked rather than floating.

Both image panes subscribe reliably, matching the Gazebo→ROS bridge's publisher. The layout lives
in `ros_ws/src/restocker_bringup/rviz/demo_camera_view.rviz` and is selected only by this demo
through the `rviz_config` launch argument; `just launch-baseline` and every headless test or
benchmark keep the default `moveit.rviz`, so nothing else changes behaviour. Depth images,
`camera_info` are not part of the layout. Gazebo's own window is separate: the showcase opens it
on the `wide` view.

The optional number limits mode-loop **cycles**, and every progress cycle submits at most one
transfer goal — so `just demo 3` moves about three products at most, not three survey-and-drain
phases. With no number the campaign continues until Ctrl-C.

## What to expect

**Showcase.** Eleven transfers fill the shelf: three cans into lane 1, three large bottles into
lane 3, two cans into lane 4 and three small bottles into lane 5. Back stock covers each deficit
with room to spare. The loop ends in `PHASE_FRONT_FULL` with the reserve blocks and the surplus of
the picked blocks still on the tray. The cold-start sweep and each transfer are slow because the
scene is large: the headless run measured 0.8 to 1.0 of real time (see the limitations below), and the
survey and three transfers of the recording took about seven minutes of wall-clock time; `just demo 3`
shows the survey and the first transfers sooner.

**Sensor-driven fixture.** Three lanes each want one product, one product per class in the tray
behind, so the loop needs **three transfers** to reach `PHASE_FRONT_FULL` with zero deficit and
then idles until evidence changes. That is the table the sensor-driven acceptance test proves. The
dense scenario's repeated products are named by camera identity too, by their stocking position,
but the dense scenario has not yet completed a sensor-driven run (see the repository README's
*Known limitations*). The dense sensor-driven end-to-end check exists as its own serialized gate,
`just dense-gate`, which is excluded by label from `just test` and `nix flake check` — a green
default suite never implies that coverage (see [Tests and benchmarks](tests-and-benchmarks)).

On every path the acceptance gate is the same shape: fill every measured deficit against the
declared targets and end in `PHASE_FRONT_FULL`, not `PHASE_STOCK_EXHAUSTED` or `PHASE_BLOCKED`.
A full dense run takes about 45 minutes; the sensor-driven fixture's three transfers take a few
minutes after the cold-start sweep. Individual transfers fail closed if motion, evidence, or
outcome verification is invalid; a blocked phase returns to surveying on the next cycle instead
of claiming success or retrying a failed pair in a tight loop.

## Current limitations of the showcase

- It runs on the ground-truth evidence path. Several SKUs of one class cannot be named by the
  sensor-driven identity path at tray and lane range yet.
- Only the twelve straight-cylinder SKUs of the generated catalogue are manipulable, and the scene
  uses five of them; the contoured bottles and boxes stay out.
- The scene is still the heaviest the simulator runs. Headless real-time factors measured on a
  32-core development machine at a load of 3 to 6: 0.8 to 0.95 for the showcase (84 products) with
  the campaign running and about 0.95 while the arm is idle. The attachment plugin used to look every
  product up by scanning the world's children three times per step and kept its pad contact request
  standing on every step, which held the same scene at 0.28; the request is now made only while a
  product is within 0.3 m of the gripper, and that stretch of a transfer is the remaining cost. The
  Gazebo window's real-time factor on a GPU was not measured.
- The lane dividers are nearly transparent, so they show only as faint edges. Shadow edges are a
  little stepped in close-ups: Gazebo exposes no shadow-map resolution.
- The robot keeps the description's materials.
- Packed columns stand on the gripper corridor pitch (about 0.13 m for cans), which is wider than
  the 12 mm between rows: the grid is tight in depth and open sideways.

Measured reliability of that loop on the sensor-driven path: a predeclared 24-run campaign on
2026-09-29 passed **14/24 (58.3 %, Wilson 95 % 38.8–75.5 %)**; every failure ended at a
named refusal (the tray survey finding no candidate, the recovery ladder ending in
`motion inhibited`, or the survey-recovery skip budget being spent).

A 24-attempt weld population passed **19/24** (79.2%, Wilson 95% 59.5–90.8%). The two populations are
not statistically separated (Fisher p=0.2124), and passing console summaries do not retain enough
detail to compare intermediate refusals. These are fixture-specific outcomes of the revisions they
were measured on, not a reliability guarantee for the current one; see
[the evidence limitations](./tests-and-benchmarks#interpret-reliability-evidence).

## Requirements

- Graphical session: `DISPLAY` or `WAYLAND_DISPLAY` (Xwayland counts)
- Built workspace (`just build`)
- Flake shell active (`direnv` or `nix develop .`)

Without a display, `scripts/demo.bash` exits immediately and points you at headless alternatives.

## Isolation

The demo leases a `ROS_DOMAIN_ID` and `GZ_PARTITION` via `scripts/domain_isolation.py`. A second
terminal that inspects topics must use the same domain the demo printed.

## Under the hood

1. Launch `baseline.launch.py` with `motion_enabled:=true`, `autonomous_campaign:=true`,
   `campaign_restart_acknowledged:=true` (the demo world is fresh, so the campaign's restart gate
   is acknowledged up front),
   `lane_policy_state:=…/demo_lane_policy_state.absent` (no persisted SetLanePolicy document can
   overlay the fixture's targets), and `rviz_config:=…/restocker_bringup/rviz/demo_camera_view.rviz`
   (the camera layout above). `just demo sensor:=true` passes **no evidence arguments at all**; the
   ground-truth paths (`just demo`, `just demo gt:=true`) additionally pass
   `object_observation_topic`, `lane_observation_topic`, both tray duties off,
   `product_observation_max_age:=2.0`, `selection_object_max_age_ms:=2000` and
   `placement_require_column_growth:=false`, and the showcase passes its own `world:=`
2. Wait up to 300 s for `/restock_action_coordinator/status` with `admission_ready: true`
3. Sweep all six front-lane stations before the first transfer; survey the tray when a deficit
   lacks an admitted candidate
4. Restock from current evidence until the front is full or no compatible pair remains, then
   repeat the scheduled survey cycle
5. Leave the GUI up until Ctrl-C or the optional maximum cycle count is reached

Showcase fixture (`showcase_products.yaml` + `showcase_lanes.yaml`): the lane policy names one
generated SKU per lane with targets of can 10, small bottle 9 and large bottle 6, short of
surveyed capacity for the same reason as the dense fixture's. The sensor-driven fixture
(`sensor_acceptance_products.yaml` + `sensor_acceptance_lanes.yaml`): lanes
`lane_01`/`lane_02`/`lane_03` each want one product, `lane_04`–`lane_06` want none, and the tray
stocks exactly one can, one small bottle and one large bottle at interleaved poses. The dense
fixture pairs its scenario with `dense_restock_lanes.yaml`, whose targets (can 10, small bottle 9,
large bottle 6 per lane) are what the shipped front columns can reach cleanly; the shipped
baseline want of six per lane is already satisfied by that overstocked fixture, so the loop would
otherwise idle immediately, and packed-capacity targets drive the last placements where the
depth-derived held count under-counts them.

Which scene a bare `just demo` runs is one line, `DEFAULT_MODE` in `scripts/demo.bash`.

Rebuild the showcase fixture with `tools/generate_showcase_scenario.py`, the dense one with
`tools/generate_dense_restock_scenario.py` and the sensor-driven one with
`tools/generate_sensor_acceptance_scenario.py` if a surveyed layout changes. To drive the
sparse baseline yourself, use [`just launch-baseline`](./scenarios) and send a `RestockProduct`
action explicitly.
