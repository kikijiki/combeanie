# Manipulators and frames

A **manipulator** is the moving arm, and here also the rail it rides on. An **end-effector**
is what touches the product (a parallel-jaw gripper). A **workcell** is the fixed surroundings:
shelf, gravity-fed lanes, stock tray, cameras. A **pose** is position plus orientation. A
**frame** is a named coordinate system bolted to part of that world (`tool0`, `shelf`,
`lane_02`).

Software never says “move to (1.2, 0.3, 0.8)” without also saying *in which frame*. Think of
frames as nested local origins in a 3D scene graph.

## Why it matters here

combeanie restocks cans and bottles from a rear stock tray into gravity-fed refrigerated shelf
lanes. The robot is a **rail + 6-DoF arm + parallel-jaw gripper**. Planning goals target
semantic datums (`grasp_center`, lane frames), not arbitrary camera pixels. Wrong frames look
like successful plans that collide or miss the product.

**Teach these names:** `world`, `rail_base`, `carriage`, `base_link`, `tool0`, `grasp_center`,
`lane_01`…`lane_06`, `*_camera_optical_frame`.

<FrameTreeDiagram />

```text
world
`-- rail_base
    `-- carriage          (rail_joint, prismatic +X)
        `-- base_link
            `-- … shoulder_pan_joint → wrist_3_joint …
                `-- flange → tool0 → grasp_center / wrist camera

world
`-- shelf → lane_01 … lane_06
         → overhead_camera_optical_frame
```

Grasp planning targets `grasp_center`; MoveIt solves to `tool0`. Units are metres and radians
(REP-103). Authoritative object poses live in `world`; transform observations at the
**acquisition** stamp, never “latest,” and never invent identity transforms.

The UR10e is selected because its 1.30 m reach covers the rail-to-stock and pre-grasp envelope;
the smaller UR3e and UR5e do not without moving the cell. The standard upstream joint names are a
cross-stack contract; there are no `joint_1`…`joint_6` compatibility aliases.
