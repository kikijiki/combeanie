# Motion planning

MoveIt 2 + OMPL (RRTConnect) plan collision-checked trajectories. The planning scene is a
**projection** of world knowledge, not the inventory database.

```text
world snapshot (+ obstacle boxes)
        │ planning_scene_projector (lease)
        ▼
/planning_scene diffs under restocker/{object,workcell,obstacle}/
        │
        ▼
move_group (OMPL) ──► MotionPort plan+execute ──► controllers
```

## Projector authority

Gate motion on `/planning_scene_projection/status`: expect `STATE_APPLIED` and an applied
revision at least as fresh as the snapshot generation you planned against. Each trajectory also
retains the projector epoch and `scene_content_generation` captured before planning. The motion
port checks that identity immediately before execution and between bounded controller goals.
A changed generation invalidates the old collision proof even when the projector is already
`APPLIED` again. The next controller goal can proceed only after collision validation against a
fresh, complete scene, including attached products, world geometry, padding and collision
permissions. Serialized projector-status service reads bracket the scene read and validation;
cached topic messages alone cannot authorize submission. Concurrent changes get at most
three attempts within the existing settle deadline. Each later slice needs its own proof against
the original provenance. This lets a harmless distant lane update progress while refusing a
changed obstacle on the route.

Validation checks the measured start, its connection to the first command, and the controller's
linear/cubic/quintic spline at 0.0025 m/rad resolution, with bounded work and cancellation checks.
This geometric check does not prove servo tracking through an allowed start-position mismatch. Missing scene data, a changed projector epoch or lease, stale evidence,
collision or exhausted validation prevents submission. Before motion this is a planning failure;
after a completed slice it is an execution failure at the stopped boundary. Scoped-contact grasp
escape retains strict generation checks because it uses a private collision matrix.

Ordinary robot motion does not change this identity. A carried product's collision body stays
fixed relative to its attachment link; its changing world pose is used for obstacle subtraction.
No-diff verification and newer world revisions preserve content generation. Attach/detach and
scene-lease release change the identity; behavioral transactions still precede independently
planned segments, and a changed scene never silently renews an earlier trajectory's proof. A held
lease requires its validated capability throughout planning and execution. Stale depth obstacles
are **retained** rather than silently forgotten, so degradation is visible.

This gate covers projector-managed geometry and authorized scene transactions. Other scene
writers must honor that ownership boundary. It is not continuous collision monitoring: changes
after a check or during an already submitted controller goal are handled at the next boundary;
a bounded goal duration does not establish dynamic-obstacle stopping clearance.

Obstacle boxes come from `depth_obstacle_node`, which `obstacle_perception` (default **on**)
composes into the baseline. They are AABBs, not an octomap.

## Candidates then MoveIt

Grasp and placement candidates are generated by deterministic geometry first
([Manipulation](./manipulation)). MoveIt only validates reachability and collision for those
goals. It does not invent grasps.

## PreInsert path corridor

Tray→lane PreInsert stays `MotionPathKind::kFreeSpace` (OMPL), not Cartesian LIN. After retract,
a short Cartesian **carry-start** moves one held-product diameter plus surveyed side clearance
toward the rear lane mouth while retaining retract X/Z/orientation. It removes the unstable OMPL
goal that reoriented beside the tray and cannot arc upward. PreInsert then owns reorientation and
rail travel under a survey-derived **AABB path constraint** expanded from its endpoints by held
product radius, planner padding, and lane side clearance. `MoveItMotionPort` applies
`setPathConstraints` for that plan only and **never** retries unconstrained on failure. Box numbers
are logged for later max-Z comparison; claiming a shorter/lower path still needs benchmark traces.
`just demo` drives the sensor acceptance fixture by default (three lanes wanting one product
each, one product per class in the tray). The labelled ground-truth opt-in `just demo gt:=true`
uses `dense_restock_products.yaml` instead: six partially stocked front lanes and three
independently ordered, dense-but-not-full back grids. The 25 back-tray products cover the 13 transfers needed
by the configured lane targets; the surplus provides slack.
