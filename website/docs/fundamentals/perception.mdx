# Perception

**RGB-D** means a color image plus a depth image from one camera (and `CameraInfo`
intrinsics). **Pose estimation** answers where a product is in 3D and which way it faces.
**Obstacle extraction** turns depth into geometric volumes MoveIt can avoid.

Perception outputs are **observations with uncertainty**, not truth. Downstream code decides
what becomes inventory evidence versus what becomes a collision box.

## Why it matters here

Two cameras exist in the description: **overhead** (obstacle detection) and **wrist**
(tray / lane survey). The shipped baseline is **sensor-driven**: the wrist confirm duty feeds
the world state's object ingest topic and the wrist lane survey its lane topic, while **Gazebo
ground truth is published** to `/perception/ground_truth/*`, which only the error evaluators read.
Ground-truth-path launches pin GT back onto the ingest topic and turn the tray duties off (one
producer per topic).

**The overhead camera is obstacles-only on the default path.** Its depth stream
feeds `depth_obstacle_node` (`obstacle_perception`, on by default), which turns unmodelled
geometry into AABB boxes projected into MoveIt. Its *object* perception — `perception:=true`,
the `perception` node running `ColourDepthBackend` — is **off** by default, so no product pose
on the default path comes from the overhead camera; that classical pipeline is optional and
**not a neural net** (chromaticity + cylinder depth fit).

The wrist camera also drives the **two-tier tray survey**: the overview duty publishes
candidate hypotheses on `/perception/tray_candidates` (never ingested by the world state),
and only the confirm duty's close-range observation lands on
`/perception/object_observations`, the topic the world state admits. The read-only
`survey_tray` action coordinates overview stations → selection → confirmation viewpoint →
confirmed or typed-refuted result; it composes `/survey_viewpoint` and adds no motion
authority of its own.

```text
wrist camera ── confirm duty (ColourDepthBackend) ─► /perception/object_observations (admitted)
wrist camera ── overview duty ────────────────────► /perception/tray_candidates
                                                    (hypotheses only; never ingested)
wrist camera ── lane survey ──────────────────────► /perception/lane_observations    (admitted)
overhead depth ── depth_obstacle_node ────────────► /perception/obstacle_observations
overhead RGB-D ── perception node                 ► /perception/object_observations
                 (perception:=true — OFF on the default path)
ground_truth_adapter ────────────────────────────► /perception/ground_truth/*
                                                    (error evaluators only)
                                    │
                                    ▼
                           world_state gates
```

When two producers publish on the same ingest topic, evidence is contested — and the world
state's single-publisher pin refuses both. That is why ground truth ships aside and every
ground-truth-path caller pins its topics explicitly (see [How to run](/docs/run/scenarios)).

Every spatial observation carries frame, acquisition stamp, backend provenance, confidence,
and covariance. Category ≠ SKU. A `LaneObservation` never rewrites `ShelfLane.contents`
directly: when the world state accepts one, it reconciles the measured column against the
FIFO ledger itself — popping front entries as sales or marking the ledger unreliable — see
[World state](/docs/architecture/world-state). A `LaneStock` is an estimate beside it: a count from the
occupied depth, a composition against the lane policy and an explicit cannot-tell, with identity named
only when label text or the barcode confirms it. The wrist survey frame shows only the rear product of a
lane, so an unflagged lane is not a verified-clean lane.

The inventory answers "how many of this SKU are in stock" from the lane stock estimates and the tray
(`inventory_tool query --sku <SKU>`). Each total is split into products an identity channel named,
products counted in a lane whose policy lists the SKU, and products only the world state reports; it
says when a lane could not be read or is older than the declared horizon, and it compares every lane
with the world state's ledger. With no identity channel running, every lane's SKU is the policy's: the
counts are exact, the SKU is an assumption, and a wrong product hidden behind the rear one is not seen.

Naming a product is a separate question from locating it. The registry and identity ladder, the
catalogue the simulator generates, and the measured results are in the
[Perception section](/docs/perception).
