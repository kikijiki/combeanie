# Introduction

combeanie is a **simulation-first** robotics project: a rail-mounted arm restocks cans and
bottles into a refrigerated retail shelf from the rear. It is vendor-neutral, and its behaviour
is checked by tests you can run.

After this page, go to [How to run](/docs/run), then [Fundamentals](/docs/fundamentals) if the
stack is new to you, then [Architecture](/docs/architecture).

## The problem

Retail beverage shelves are restocked from behind the display: staff open the cabinet back,
place cans and bottles into gravity-fed lanes, and products roll forward to the customer face.
That work is repetitive, happens in a tight corridor behind the fridge, and must keep lanes
sorted by product without wedging or tipping stock.

combeanie studies that job in simulation first: pick from a stock tray, plan a safe motion,
place into a compatible lane mouth, and check the outcome in world state. If the outcome cannot
be verified, the task refuses instead of guessing.

A restock succeeds only when world state shows the product in the intended lane. Collisions, lost reservations, stale
geometry, and ambiguous perception stop the task.

Out of scope for now: physical store hardware, learned policies that command joints directly,
and a single launch that turns every optional subsystem on without saying so.

## The scenario

The default simulated cell is a rear-access refrigerated display:

| Element | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Gravity-fed shelf | Six lanes behind a front retainer; roller bed inclined about 4° so stock settles at the customer face |
| Stock tray | Staging area behind the shelf where products wait to be grasped |
| Rail-mounted arm | Travels along a rail behind the shelf so one arm covers every lane |
| Cameras | Overhead RGB-D mast (obstacle detection only) plus wrist camera (the sensor-driven default's product and lane evidence) |

Products in the catalog are cans and bottles of a few sizes. Lanes are paired by class so the
task has a choice when one lane is full or blocked. Geometry comes from surveyed YAML, not
invented mesh sizes.

World-frame plan with `rail_base` at the origin and the shelf at the baseline workcell pose
(`0, 0.55, 0.75` m). The arm stands behind the shelf, between the stock tray and the lane mouths.

## The robot

One arm covers the whole shelf by riding a horizontal rail behind the display. A carriage on
that rail carries a six-axis manipulator and a parallel-jaw gripper. The planning tip is
`tool0`; grasp geometry is centered on `grasp_center`, a fixed offset beyond the tool flange.
A decorative beanie (hence the name) sits on the shoulder cap: visual only, with no
collision, no mass, no effect on planning or perception.

Side (YZ) and front (XZ) views in the zero pose. Rail span is 4.0 m.
`grasp_center` sits 0.14 m beyond `tool0` along the gripper axis.

![Robot orthographic schematic](/img/schematics/robot-orthographic.svg)

| Frame | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `rail_base` | Fixed rail origin in the world |
| `carriage` | Sliding mount that carries the arm |
| `tool0` | MoveIt / control tip frame |
| `grasp_center` | Where fingers close on a product |
| `beanie` | Decorative shoulder-cap beanie (visual only) |

Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, reset the camera from the toolbar. Switch between home
(upright) and a reach toward `lane_03`. Labels follow the visual components in each pose; they mark mesh centers, not TF origins.
The [frame tree](/docs/architecture/workcell-and-frames) describes frame ancestry.

## Stack

```text
Gazebo Harmonic  +  ROS 2 Jazzy  +  ros2_control  +  MoveIt 2
        │
        └── Nix flake pins the toolchain; just drives build/test/launch
```

No host ROS install is required. Physical hardware is out of scope for this version.
