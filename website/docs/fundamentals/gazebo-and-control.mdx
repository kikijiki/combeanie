# Gazebo and ros2_control

**Gazebo Harmonic** is the physics simulator (gz-sim 8): rigid bodies, contacts, cameras,
custom system plugins. It is the *physical* ground truth for the baseline.

**ros2_control** is the standard control stack: a **controller manager** loads controllers
(joint trajectory, gripper position, joint-state broadcaster) that read/write hardware
interfaces.

**gz_ros2_control** is the bridge plugin: simulated joints *look like* a real robot to
ros2_control. Controllers do not care whether the “motor” is metal or a physics engine.

## Why it matters here

MoveIt does not talk to Gazebo directly:

```text
MoveIt trajectory
  → FollowJointTrajectory controllers
  → gz_ros2_control
  → Gazebo joints
```

Grasping uses a **custom Gazebo attachment system** (detachable joint) plus a ROS adapter,
because beverage restock needs carry semantics beyond open/close fingers. The baseline uses wrist RGB-D confirmation for operational object poses and wrist surveys
for lane evidence. The **ground-truth adapter** publishes separate evaluator topics by default;
it is an explicit opt-in operational source.

Arm and rail controllers are **velocity** (+ feed-forward); the gripper is **position**
(simulation lag on the gripper is expected). The simulation clock arrives on `/clock`: use it for acquisition stamps, TF lookups,
observation freshness and controller timing. Operation and whole-task budgets deliberately use
a steady clock, so a paused or slow simulation can still exceed a deadline. Record both clocks
when investigating a timeout.
