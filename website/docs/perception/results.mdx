# Results

Each section is one measurement of the perception code as it stands: what was measured, on what
data, against which bounds (committed before the held-out data was read), the outcome, and what the
measurement does not cover. Failed and inconclusive bounds are stated in the same terms as passes.
Method is on [Benchmarks](./benchmarks). Each section names the project measurement record it comes
from.

Every number is from Gazebo renders with ideal depth (plus a stated synthetic noise model where
noted), fictional labels and the generated catalogue. None is a measurement of a real camera or
real packaging.

| Measurement | Held-out data | Bounds | Passed | Failed | Inconclusive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| [Proposals, boxes](#proposals-boxes) | 36 scenes | 9 | 8 | 0 | 1 |

## Proposals

*Record: `2026-10-19-proposal-benchmark`.* Question: is depth geometry alone enough to propose every
product in tray and lane scenes, including cluttered trays, lying products, contoured bottles and
partly hidden products?

- **Data:** 48 held-out scenes (8 per tray kind: sparse, dense, tight rows, lying, contour, tall
  products in front of short ones), 287 wrist frames, 771 recallable products, 1467 proposals.
  Bounds in `proposal_acceptance_bounds.json`, proposer configuration frozen in
  `proposal_config_frozen.json` (disc and blob shapes), one run.
- **Outcome:** every bound passed.

| Bound | Result | Scene-clustered 95 % interval | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Overall strict recall at least 0.90 (lower limit at least 0.85) | 0.939 | [0.915, 0.962] | pass |
| Lying products, same bound (lower limit at least 0.80) | 0.961 | [0.91, 1.00] | pass |
| Contoured bottle | 0.966 | [0.93, 1.00] | pass |
| Touching (gap under 3 mm) | 0.934 | [0.90, 0.96] | pass |
| Partial occlusion (0.6 to 0.9 visible) | 0.915 | [0.87, 0.95] | pass |
| Lane | 0.953 | [0.91, 0.99] | pass |
| Cluttered trays (dense, rows, tall in front) | 0.908 | [0.871, 0.940] | pass, 0.008 above its bound |
| Foreign proposals at most 0.05 | 0.0075 (11 of 1467) | [0.004, 0.013] | pass |
| Latency p95 at most 500 ms | 163 ms (p50 90 ms) | | pass |

Reported without a bound: loose recall 0.948, merged proposals 4.0 %, strict precision 0.49 (about
1.9 proposals per recallable product: hidden products and cut plateaus fragment into extra proposals
that overlap a real product), mean matched IoU 0.85. The weakest strata, all passing, are tray kind
`rows` at 0.862 and dim light at 0.910.

**Identity at the proposal.** Through the `/identify_product` channel (one crop per proposal, embedding
and label text), held-out proposals with at least 1500 pixels gave:

| Proposal covers | n | Named | Named correctly | Named wrongly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A registered product | 691 | 299 | 171 | 128 |
| An unregistered product | 343 | 215 | 0 | 215 (62.7 %) |
| Neither (a fragment) | 16 | 12 | 0 | 12 |

A consumer must not act on `IDENTIFIED` from one proposal crop. The state guarantees that no SKU is
forced beyond the channel's own answer (0 unknown-product states carried a SKU in this data).

**Not covered:** the production colour-and-depth backend (its colour stage cannot find label-textured
products); real depth noise, shiny or transparent packaging; foreign shapes such as bags; masks other
than the analytic ones. Lane products at the home pose are visible only at the image edge.

### Proposals, boxes \{#proposals-boxes\}

rectangle shape. Bounds in `box_proposal_acceptance_bounds.json`; data: 36 held-out box scenes
(seeds 2101 to 2136), 216 frames, one run, real Gazebo recordings. **Decision: inconclusive** (8
checks pass, none fails, one check lacked the required sample).

| Check | Bound | Result | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Boxes, strict recall | at least 0.85 | 0.940 (497 / 529) | pass |
| Lying boxes | at least 0.70 | 0.805 (stratum 120 / 144 = 0.833) | pass |
| Upright boxes | at least 0.90 | 0.980 | pass |
| Matched boxes named a cylinder | at most 0.08 | 0.028 | pass |
| Cylinders | at least 0.90 | 1.000 (can 66/66, small bottle 49/49, large bottle 35/35) | pass |
| Lying cylinders | at least 0.85 | 1.000 | **inconclusive**: fewer than the required 30 lying cylinders |
| Cylinders matched as a rectangle | at most 0.03 | 0.007 | pass |

| Latency p95 | at most 500 ms | 281 ms | pass |

Overall strict recall is 0.953 [0.94, 0.97] (647 / 679). The weak spots are lying products (0.833),
touching products (0.921) and heavy occlusion (0.915). **Not covered:** the box scene builder uses the
circumscribed circle of each box's footprint as its footprint, so box neighbours are never in true
contact and the "touching" stratum for boxes is in that circle metric.

## Lane stock

*Record: `2026-10-19-lane-stock-estimator-round2`, with the identity arms of
`2026-10-19-lane-stock-estimator`.* Question: from the frame the wrist survey takes of a lane, can the
system say how many products are in it, say it cannot when it cannot see, and flag a wrong or mixed
lane, with the identity channel treated as possibly abstaining?

- **Data:** 450 held-out lanes per arm (75 fresh seeds, six lighting conditions), one wrist frame per
  lane survey station, all views reached. Bounds in `lane_stock_acceptance_bounds_r2.json`, estimator
  files hashed before the run, each arm run once.
- **Outcome:** all 19 bounds pass.

| Bound | Arm | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Count within one on sufficient-coverage lanes at least 0.90 | abstain | 450 / 450 (exact on all) |
| Clean lanes flagged misplaced at most 5 % | abstain, oracle | 0 / 338, 0 / 338 |
| Visible wrong product flagged given a perfect channel at least 85 % | oracle | 56 / 56 |
| Low-coverage lanes refused at least 90 % | degrade | 209 / 209 |
| Answered-wrong at most 3 % | degrade | 0 / 238 |
| Clean lanes flagged at most 5 % | degrade | 0 / 181 |
| Answered at least 90 % | noise 0.5 %, 1 % | 449 / 450, 448 / 450 |
| Answered at least 85 % | noise 2 % | 413 / 450 (91.8 %) |
| Answered-wrong at most 3 % | noise 0.5 %, 1 %, 2 % | 0 / 449, 0 / 448, 0 / 413 |
| Empty lanes read empty at least 90 % | noise 0.5 %, 1 %, 2 % | 82 / 82, 82 / 82, 80 / 82 |
| Clean lanes flagged at most 5 % | noise 0.5 %, 1 %, 2 % | 0 / 337, 0 / 337, 0 / 314 |

At 2 % noise 8 % of lanes are cannot-tells (all `REASON_INCONSISTENT_GEOMETRY`) and the estimator
makes no class claim.

**Misplaced detection is limited by what the camera sees and by the identity channel, not by the
estimator.** With the `/identify_product` channel under the safe naming policy, none of 382 lane rear
products was named: at lane range (1.0 to 1.4 m) every answer was embedding-alone, label text and the
barcode never confirmed one, and the embedding alone was wrong two times in three; it named a
registered SKU on all 81 lanes whose rear product was unregistered. Misplaced recall for the system as built is **0 / 132**
wrong-SKU lanes. A perfect channel would reach 53 / 132 (40.2 %), because a survey frame shows only the
rearmost product, and 53 / 53 when the wrong product is the visible one. A lane the estimator did not
flag is not a verified-clean lane.

**Not covered:** a real camera's noise structure (range-dependent bias, flying pixels, multipath on the
translucent dividers); mixed-class columns, which only ray-cast unit tests cover; frames beyond a
single camera pose per lane; the numpy depth port against the C++ node on the same recorded frames.

### Lane stock, boxes under noise \{#lane-stock-boxes\}

*Record: `2026-10-20-box-lane-noise-round2`.* Data: 56 held-out seeds (309 to 364), 336 lane frames,
137 box lanes and 56 empty lanes, five arms, one run. Bounds in `lane_stock_acceptance_bounds_noise120.json`.
**17 bounds pass, none fails, 4 are inconclusive** because the population fell short of the declared
sample sizes (137 box lanes against 150 required, 297 answered lanes against 300).

| Bound | Result | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Box lanes exact on sufficient coverage at least 0.80 (n at least 150) | 134 / 137 = 0.978 [0.938, 0.993] | inconclusive: n 137 |
| Box answered-wrong at most 0.05 (n at least 150) | 0 / 134 [0, 0.028] | inconclusive: n 134 |
| All lanes within one at least 0.90 | 333 / 336 = 0.991 | pass |
| All answered-wrong at most 0.03 | 0 / 333 [0, 0.011] | pass |
| Empty lanes read empty at least 0.75 | 56 / 56 | pass |
| Clean lanes flagged at most 0.10 | 0 / 255 | pass |
| Degrade: low coverage refused at least 0.90 | 161 / 161 | pass |
| Degrade: answered-wrong at most 0.05 (box 0.10) | 0 / 169 (box 0 / 73) | pass |
| Answered at least 0.85: noise 0.5 %, 1 % | 333 / 336, 324 / 336 | pass |
| Answered at least 0.80: noise 2 % | 297 / 336 = 0.884 | pass |
| Answered-wrong at most 0.03: noise 0.5 %, 1 % | 0 / 333, 0 / 324 | pass |
| Answered-wrong at most 0.03: noise 2 % | 0 / 297 [0, 0.013] | inconclusive: n 297 |
| Box answered-wrong at most 0.05: noise 0.5 %, 1 % | 0 / 134, 0 / 126 | pass |
| Box answered-wrong at most 0.05: noise 2 % (n at least 120) | 0 / 102 [0, 0.036] | inconclusive: n 102 |
| Box exact among answered at least 0.80: noise 0.5 %, 1 % | 133 / 134, 125 / 126 | pass |
| Empty lanes read empty: noise 0.5 %, 1 % | 56 / 56, 56 / 56 | pass |

No inconclusive row has a failing point estimate (all had 0 wrong answers). Box lanes exact on
sufficient coverage: 133 / 137 at 0.5 % noise, 125 / 137 (0.912) at 1 % and 94 / 137 (0.686) at 2 %,
the rest refused or one product off. All refusals are inconsistent geometry.

**Not covered:** a box turned a quarter turn in its lane is not a candidate and is not detected; a lane
packed that way would be mis-counted or refused, and the simulator never produces one. The lane's own
product wins among height matches, so a different box of nearly the same height (slim box against 1 L
carton at 1 % noise or more) is counted with the lane's product's pitch unless the residual refuses it.
The noise is the synthetic Gaussian model on an ideal render.

## `/identify_product` channel \{#identify-product\}

*Record: `2026-10-19-identity-channel-round2`.* The channel fuses a SigLIP 2 base embedding, label text
and barcode with calibrated abstention. Data: held-out fresh seeds, one decision per object with up to
five views, 234 known and 78 unregistered objects. Bounds in `identity_acceptance_bounds_r2.json`.

| Bound | Result | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| False identity on known products at most 1 % | 3 / 234 = 1.28 % [0.44, 3.70] | **fail** (point estimate above the bound) |
| Unregistered products named as a SKU at most 5 % | 34 / 78 = 43.6 % [33.1, 54.6] | **fail** |
| p95 latency at most 2000 ms | 1977 ms (p50 1301 ms) | pass, with load average 15 to 16 on the host |

Both sample-size requirements were met, so the bounds are refuted, not merely undemonstrated.
Of the 34 unregistered products named, 33 were embedding-alone names of an unregistered twin of a
registered single product. Per channel on known products: the embedding answers always (214 / 234
correct), label text answered 18 / 234 (all correct), the barcode 53 / 234 (all correct). Dropping
embedding-alone naming from the held-out decisions would have named 61 / 234 correctly, with none wrong
and 1 / 78 unregistered products named; that is a post-hoc figure, not a result. It is the reason the
lane stock estimator accepts only names that label text or the barcode confirm, and the reason the
identity ladder uses the embedding as a shortlist only.

**Not covered:** the barcode reads only at 0.22 m with the aimed orbit and a code that spans 137 degrees
of the circumference, which real labels do not.

## Identity ladder

*Record: `2026-10-21-identity-text-reading-r4`.* The ladder, with its optional second text reader, is
described on [Registry and identity](./registry-and-identity). Data: 240 fresh held-out seeds, 965 tray
objects (892 of registered products, 73 unregistered look-alike twins), a registry of 18 products, up to
three re-looks through the rotation oracle. Bounds in `identity_ladder_acceptance_bounds_r4.json`, committed
before the first held-out scene was recorded; the headline arm is barcode off, with the aimed barcode
close-ups left out as well, because a product without a barcode never gets them. One run.

| Bound | Barcode off (headline) |
| --- | --- |
| False identity on named at most 1 % (n at least 400) | 0 / 672, upper limit 0.57 %: pass |
| Unregistered named at most 10 % (n at least 40) | 1 / 73, upper limit 7.4 %: pass |
| Coverage of known objects at least 0.60 | **75.3 % (672 / 892), lower limit 72.4 %: pass** |
| Mean re-looks of named objects at most 1.2 | 1.01: pass |
| p95 re-looks at most 3 | 3: pass |
| Latency p95 per look at most 4000 ms (second reader excluded) | 3025 ms: pass |
| Latency p95 per escalated read at most 40 s | 23.6 s: pass |
| Wrong after a re-look / after a second-reader read, at most 2 each | 0 / 0: pass |

**The headline arm passes every bound.** Coverage of known objects by number of re-looks: 29.6 % at look 0,
then 52.2 %, 68.3 % and 75.3 %. The second reader named 389 of the 672 named objects on its own (548 after a
second-reader read). No twin was confused with its registered pair, and the one unregistered object that was
named (`sim:stock_small_bottle_01`, text rung, second look) is the look-alike case the bounds warn about.
By class: box 400 / 495, can 118 / 155, large bottle 74 / 123, small bottle 80 / 119; lying products
115 / 119, upright 557 / 773.

Two comparison arms were measured on the same objects and are reported as they came out:

| Arm | Coverage | Unknown named | Latency p95 per look |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Barcode off, aimed views kept | 72.0 %, lower limit 68.9 % (bound 0.65): pass | 4.1 %, upper limit 11.4 %: inconclusive | 4889 ms: **fail** |
| Barcode on | 74.6 %, lower limit 71.6 % (bound 0.70): pass | 8.2 %, upper limit 16.8 %: inconclusive | 4916 ms: **fail** |

The barcode-on arm named one object wrongly (1 / 665, upper limit 0.85 %, under the bound). With only 73
unregistered objects, the unregistered-named bound cannot be decided for these two arms.

Guidance against the simulator's truth (1522 estimates): round bodies median error 26.8 degrees, 52.5 %
within 30 degrees and 64.7 % within 45; boxes median 63.8 degrees, 22.5 % within 30 and 36.2 % within 45.

**Two machines.** The 240 scenes were processed on two CPU-only machines, a shared workstation and a laptop.
The headline arm passes both latency bounds on each separately (laptop 3.44 s per look and 8.7 s per
second-reader read, workstation 1.60 s and 24.5 s), so the pooled verdict is not an artefact of mixing. The
two comparison arms fail the per-look bound on the laptop alone (6.65 s there, 2.8 s on the workstation). The
second reader runs about three times slower on the workstation, which was loaded; the cause was not isolated.
Look 0 of three seeds recorded on both machines gave the same identification for every object visible on both,
but a different set of visible objects, about as different as two recordings on one machine. Three seeds
cannot rule out an effect of the hardware.

**Not covered:** the unregistered products are twins of registered products and mostly bottles, so the
unregistered-named rate for boxes is not bounded. On a replay with the true product removed, text alone
named an unregistered twin for 6 of 91 objects, mostly boxes of one look-alike pair. Re-looks use the
rotation oracle, since no manipulation skill turns a product. The scenes were recorded in simulation with
generated labels. The run was interrupted by lost scratch data, pauses on the shared machine and a move to
the laptop; the record lists each interruption, and one correction to the benchmark's bookkeeping (an
open barcode-on arm with no guidance now asks for no turn). It does not change any decision of the
headline arm.

### Box identity, development diagnostic

*Record: `2026-10-21-box-products-diagnostic`.* No bound was declared and none is claimed. On train
seeds, with every box registered, the `/identify_product` channel named 16 of 16 box objects correctly
(34 of 34 single views), with no barcode decoded in any of the 31 groups. The registry's box views come
from the atlas the simulator shows, so this says the renderer and the simulator agree. The behaviour on
an unregistered box was not measured.

## Inventory

*Record: `2026-10-20-inventory`.* Question: does the inventory layer over lane stock report per-SKU
totals, staleness and discrepancy against the world state correctly, and does it refuse to claim what
the evidence does not support?

- **Data:** the 450 lane survey frames of the lane stock held-out seeds, replayed through the frozen
  predictions, with the world state as the labelled truth, optionally perturbed by a declared seeded
  schedule (ledger counts off by one or two, a third of lanes; rearmost SKU swapped, a quarter of
  lanes). Bounds in `inventory_acceptance_bounds.json`. One run. **All 33 bound rows pass.**
- These seeds were scored for the estimator before; the inventory code, bounds and perturbation schedule
  were set on 115 development seeds (690 lanes).

| Bound | Arm | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complete SKU total within one product at least 0.95 | abstain / oracle | 0.978 / 0.996 (459 pairs each) |
| | noise 0.5 %, 1 %, 2 % | 0.978, 0.978, 0.984 |
| | degrade | 0.980 (297) |
| SKU with a refused policy lane reported complete at most 3 % | degrade | 0 / 162 |
| Confirmed SKU contradicted by the labels at most 2 % | oracle | 0 / 368 lanes |
| Lane older than the horizon flagged stale at least 95 % | abstain, oracle, degrade | 145 / 145 |
| Lane inside the horizon flagged stale at most 2 % | same | 0 / 305 |
| Totals with a stale lane equal totals without it at least 90 % | same | 68 / 68 scenes |
| Perturbed ledger count flagged at least 90 % | abstain, oracle, noise 0.5 %, 1 % | 149 / 149 |
| | noise 2 % / degrade | 129 / 133 (0.970) / 71 / 71 |
| Truthful ledger flagged at most 3 % | abstain, oracle | 0 / 301 |
| | noise 0.5 %, 1 %, 2 % / degrade | 0 / 300, 0 / 299, 0 / 280 / 0 / 167 |
| Refused lane reported not comparable at least 97 % | degrade | 212 / 212 |
| Swapped ledger SKU flagged given the rear was named at least 90 % | oracle | 89 / 89 |
| Truthful ledger SKU contradicted at most 3 % | abstain, oracle | 0 / 361 |

Reported without a bound: the SKU total is exactly right in 0.604 of the cases without identity and
0.787 with a perfect channel, and the range `total_min` to `total_max` contains the labelled total in the
same fractions. A wrong product hidden behind the rear one is invisible to a survey frame and unnamed
products are assumed to be the lane policy's SKU, so per-SKU totals are wrong by one in about 40 % of
(seed, SKU) pairs. A swapped ledger SKU is detected only when the rear product is named: for 0 of 89 lanes
without an identity channel and 89 of 89 with a perfect one.

**Not covered:** tray perception (the benchmark's wrist frames at home show nothing and the overhead
frames are black, so the tray is the world state's); the staleness results test the age logic, with ages
from a seeded schedule against a declared 600 s horizon, not a measured cadence; real ledger errors (a
sale the world state missed, a product moved by a person); the node against the live simulator.

## Not measured \{#not-measured\}

- **Real sensors.** No real camera, no real depth noise (flying pixels, range-dependent bias,
  multipath), no real lighting, no shiny or transparent packaging.
- **Real labels.** All labels are generated, with large upright text and ideal print. Real packaging,
  curved reflective print and barcodes that do not span 137 degrees are untested.
- **The live stack.** The lane stock and inventory nodes were exercised in one-process graphs with fake
  upstream nodes and replayed predictions, not against a running simulator with
  `lane_stock:=true inventory:=true`.
- **The tray.** Inventory totals use a world-sourced tray; tray perception with identity state is
  covered by unit and node tests only.
- **Manipulation of what perception sees.** No skill rotates a product, so the ladder's guidance has
  no executor. No box is grasped, placed or inserted into a lane.
- **Quarter-turned boxes and columns.** A box turned in its lane is not detected.
- **Foreign shapes.** Bags and other non-catalogue shapes, and unregistered pairs of look-alikes, are
  not in any scene.
- **Mixed-class columns** in the benchmark: the staircase logic is covered by ray-cast unit tests.
- **The overhead camera.** Its benchmark frames are almost entirely black; it is not scored.
- **Closer aimed lane views.** Lane products are further from the rail than the camera can come to
  reading range; lane-range identity is as measured above.
